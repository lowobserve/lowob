What Is UGC, and Why Brands Now Pay for It

By lowob · 3 August 2026 · 5-minute read · Growth

UGC now means two things: genuine customer content, credible because it was not commissioned, and paid creator content styled to look organic, which performs in paid social. Use both, keep them distinct, and disclose and review the paid kind as the advertising it is.

User-generated content, UGC, originally meant exactly what it says: content made by users, customers posting photos, reviews, and videos about products on their own initiative. Brands loved it because it was credible and free.

The term has since been adopted by the advertising industry to describe something different: content produced by paid creators, deliberately styled to look like an ordinary person's post, for use in the brand's own advertising. The two meanings now coexist, and the distinction matters for both effectiveness and law.

The two kinds

Organic UGC is made by real customers, unpaid, on their own accounts. Brands can request permission to reuse it, feature it, and reward it, and it remains the most credible form of social proof available because it was not commissioned.

Commissioned UGC-style content is made by creators paid to produce it, usually not posted on the creator's own account at all but delivered to the brand for use in paid ads. The creator's following is irrelevant; what the brand is buying is the format, a person on a phone talking about the product the way a friend would, because that format performs in paid social far better than polished production does.

Why brands pay for it

Paid social platforms reward content that looks native to the feed. Studio-produced ads are identified and skipped; a person holding a phone is watched. Commissioned UGC-style content is cheap to produce in volume, fast to iterate, and allows dozens of variations to be tested against one another. For a performance program that needs new creative every few weeks, it has become the default.

What it costs

Per piece, from a modest fee for a short video from a newer creator to considerably more from experienced UGC creators with a track record of producing ads that convert. Platforms and marketplaces exist to source and manage creators at scale. Usage rights, how long and where the brand may run the content, are priced separately and are often the larger cost.

The disclosure question

When commissioned content runs as the brand's own ad, in the brand's ad account, it is an advertisement and is treated as one; the person in it is effectively an actor, and the platform labels it as sponsored. Claims made in it are the brand's claims and must meet the same standards as any ad, including, in reviewed categories, the same compliance review.

When a paid creator posts UGC-style content on their own account presenting it as their own opinion, it is an endorsement, and the material connection must be disclosed clearly and conspicuously under the endorsement rules that apply in most jurisdictions. The "it looks organic" quality that makes the format effective is precisely what the disclosure rules exist to address.

Testimonial content also carries its own rules: claims about results must reflect what a typical customer can expect, or say otherwise, and fabricated or scripted testimonials presented as genuine are prohibited.

Using it well

Brief creators on the product truth and the audience's actual doubts, and let them speak naturally; scripted UGC reads as scripted. Produce in volume and test; the format's advantage is iteration. Keep organic UGC separate and honest: feature real customers, with permission, and never edit their words into claims they did not make. Apply the compliance library to every piece, because a creator saying "I earned twelve percent" is a yield claim whoever says it. And in restricted categories, remember that the content still has to run somewhere; UGC-style creative does not open a platform that has closed the category.

UGC now means two things: genuine customer content, which is credible because it was not commissioned, and paid creator content styled to look organic, which performs in paid social. Use both, keep them distinct, and disclose and review the paid kind as the advertising it is.
